# Full worker, profiles and one budget pool

How `apps/elise-engine-api` runs a Scan with the `full` Skill, how a Scan's Budget cap is set from its Profile and spent, and who may run one. Terms: `apps/elise-engine-api/GLOSSARY.md` (Full worker, Profile, Phase, Runner, Partial Scan, Budget cap, Pool, Verdict weight, Outbound handler). A Step is still cloning, auditing or saving; a Phase is a part of the audit and is never called a Step.

## Two workers inside one Worker

- **There is one path: the full one.** A full Scan has its own Workflow (`FullScanWorkflow`), its own Sandbox class (`FullScanSandbox`) and its own Image (`Dockerfile.full`). The lite Skill, Workflow, Sandbox class and Image were removed (#141). The reason they were separate while both existed: a full Scan runs for an hour or more and holds a Sandbox the whole time, so on shared classes it would have taken the place of lite Scans.
- **Every new Scan takes the full path, a Scan on a Runner included.** The request's `skill` field is ignored (`create-scan-svc.ts`; #146 removes it). Old lite rows are only read: no code records a Scan of that Skill's findings, writes the History, or gives an agent Prior findings (#178), and the Workflow has no `read-priors` Step. The rows, the skill's value and its display name stay, so an old Scan still opens, shows its diff, counts in a Verdict and has its findings on its Branch.
- **It stays one Worker.** Two Workers would mean two deploys, two sets of secrets and two copies of the `Outbound` code that holds the Anthropic key. Separate classes give the isolation we need without that.
- **Limits are separate too.** A full Scan has a time limit for each Phase (10, 45, 25 and 10 minutes, 90 in all) and counts as live for 130 minutes without a status change. Each path has its own count of live Scans for each Account (`MAX_ACTIVE_FULL_SCANS`, 1). There is no count over all Accounts: one Account's Scan never refuses another's. A new Scan over its Account's limit is refused with `TOO_MANY_ACTIVE_SCANS` and no queue. We do not queue because a queue hides a wait that the person cannot see and cannot cancel.

## The Budget cap comes from the Profile

- **Three profiles.** `quick`, `standard` and `deep` (the dashboard says Focused, Balanced and Exhaustive). No profile means `quick`. The dashboard form starts on `standard`.
- **The caps are a table, and they are guesses.** `SCAN_PROFILE_CAPS_USD` (`src/constants/scan-constants.ts`) gives $12, $24 and $50. They stay provisional until the first real Scans (#142) are measured. They were $6, $12 and $18 until #414: a real Focused Scan of this repository on a Runner spent $1.70 on `recon` and $4.04 on `hunt`, and ended with "the budget cap of $6 was reached during the check phase" a minute into `check`. Exhaustive's $50 is the Runner's own ceiling (`RUNNER_MAX_BUDGET_USD`), so no Runner release is needed. The cloud's credit hold (cap times the markup) rises with the table; nobody runs the cloud path today, and #142 revisits it.
- **A request may lower the cap, never raise it.** `MAX_BUDGET_FULL_USD` ($50 today, so Exhaustive's cap fits) is the ceiling that guards the table: a profile whose cap is above it refuses its Scans, so a wrong table never starts one. `MAX_BUDGET_USD`, `MAX_BUDGET_OWN_KEY_USD` and `MAX_GLOBAL_LITE_SCANS` went with the lite path. A Runner Scan keeps `RUNNER_MAX_BUDGET_USD` as its own ceiling.
- **A Runner Scan's request may raise the limit, up to the Runner ceiling (#415).** The Scan's limit is the request's `max_budget_usd`, else its Profile's cap, and a request above `RUNNER_MAX_BUDGET_USD` ($50, held in the contracts as `RUNNER_SPEND_LIMIT_MAX_USD`) is refused. The stored `maxBudgetUsd` is that limit, and the claimed job sends it as both `maxBudgetUsd` and `profileCapUsd`, so an older Runner, which takes the lower of the two, also honors a raised limit and needs no release. Why: the Scan runs on the person's own Claude and takes no credit, so a higher limit changes nothing Elise charges, and a person who sees a large repo reach its cap should be able to give it more. Cloud Scans keep the rule above. A Runner Scan queued before this change holds $50 as its `maxBudgetUsd` and now runs under $50, not its Profile's cap; the ceiling is the same one it always had. `RUNNER_SPEND_LIMIT_MAX_USD` (contracts) and the Runner app's `BUDGET_CAP_USD` are two copies of that $50, so raising one means raising both, and the Runner release goes first.
- **Credit.** A new Scan on the platform's key holds the cap it was given times the Plan's markup (1.5). The Account is charged only what the meter counted, times the markup. `GET /usage/credit` returns each profile's cap and hold.

## The Budget cap is one pool

- **One meter for the Scan.** The four Phases (`recon`, `hunt`, `check` and `report`) run in order as separate runs of the Agent and spend from one meter that holds the whole cap (`scan-sandbox/utils/meter.ts`). No Phase has a share of its own, and nothing moves between Phases.
- **No Report reserve.** When the pool is spent the meter refuses each new model call. A Partial Scan with a Report happens only when the Skill reads the budget message while it can still write its files: it then marks unfinished units `deferred` with `money_exhausted`, and the Report starts with `PARTIAL:`. A Phase that ends cleanly goes on to the next one.
- **A Scan that spends its whole pool** ends with the error `the budget cap of $N was reached during the P phase`, and writes no Report. It is charged its metered cost times the markup, as any failed Scan with a recorded cost is. This risk is accepted: #142 counts how many real Scans end this way, and a Report reserve is added if it is common.
- **Runner Scans have no server meter.** The Agent stops at the cap through its own budget limit, and the Skill gets no budget message, so the Skill never writes the partial files itself. The Runner app sends them for it (see "A Runner Scan a limit stops is a Partial Scan" under Runner Scans). How the Runner holds the pool is under Runner Scans. #142 counts how often.

## A Partial Scan keeps its Report

A Scan that runs out of money while the Skill can still write its files ends with a Report and a findings file. Both say it is partial and name the units that were not covered. It is charged only what was metered. The reason: a Scan that ends in an error with no Report loses the money already spent and tells the person nothing. A Partial Scan's Report with its gaps named is something a person can act on, and they can start another Scan for what was missed. Whether the Scan was partial is saved on its row when it is recorded (`scans.partial`), so the Verdict reads that column, not the Report. A Partial Scan weighs 0 in a Verdict and its findings still count as open (ADR 0003).

## Who may run a Scan

- **A Scan on the platform's key needs a Plan.** An Account with no Plan and no key of its own is refused with `NO_ACTIVE_PLAN`. `BILLING_ENFORCED` keeps its own, wider rule (it also covers own-key and Runner Scans) and is `false` today. An Account with no Plan that has its own key runs on that key, as before. Own-key Scans and Runner Scans are not refused for lack of a Plan. This ships before profile Scans open on the platform's key (#135 before #134), so no Scan runs on the platform's key without a credit hold.
- **The Monthly budget still applies once an admin sets one on a Plan.** A Scan whose hold would pass it is refused, and the message names the biggest smaller profile that would fit, or says the budget is too low for any profile.
- **Tenant isolation is checked before profile Scans reach the platform's key.** Row-level security on the remaining tenant tables and the two-Account isolation test (#60) must pass before #134 is deployed. Full Scans keep more of a customer's code in one place for longer, so a leak between Accounts costs more.

## Reversal: measurement no longer comes first

- **This reverses the measure-first rule.** An earlier version of this ADR held full Scans on the platform's key until the measurement (#69) had set the full ceiling and the minimum cap. That rule is reversed. Why: the Plan rule and the cap give a bounded loss. A platform-key Scan needs a Plan and holds its cap times the markup, so a wrong guess costs at most the cap per Scan. The first real Scans (#142) take the place of the measurement, and the caps stay guesses until then.
- **The isolation test stays a gate.** #60 must pass before #134 is deployed, and the owner checks it by hand.

## Runner Scans

- **The Runner's Scan rules live in one shared core, so they apply to both Runners (#184).** The claim, the clone, the Phases on one Pool, the validators, the repair run, collecting and sending the files, and the agent's settings are in `apps/elise-runner-cli/core` (`elise-runner-core`, no Tauri dependency, mainly `scan.rs`). The desktop app and the headless Runner (`elise-runner`) are shells over it: each gives the core where `claude`, `node` and the Skill are, and turns the core's events into its own output. Everything below holds for both. A change to a rule here lands in the core once. "The app" below means whichever Runner runs the Scan.
- **A stopped Runner reports its Scan as failed (#188).** On Ctrl-C (`SIGINT`) or `SIGTERM` during a Scan, the headless Runner tells the core to stop. The running `claude`, clone or validator is killed, no further Phase starts, and the Scan goes through the usual failed path: Elise gets a failed result whose error says the Runner was stopped, with the agent's log so far, and the work folder is kept. With no Scan running the program exits at once. Why: a Scan whose Runner is told to stop would otherwise stay `running` in the dashboard until the engine's wait ran out. The desktop app never stops a Scan midway: quitting it kills `claude` and sends no result. What the engine does for a Runner that disappears without reporting is #171, not this rule.
- **The agent may not read `/proc`, and never gets the Runner's token (#184).** The settings the core writes deny reading the system's process folder, and the core removes `ELISE_RUNNER_TOKEN` from the environment of every `claude` it starts. Both are in the core, so they hold for the desktop app too.
- **A Runner Scan runs the four Phases (#156, #158).** This reverses an earlier decision here, that a Runner Scan runs one Agent, not Phases, and that a Runner cannot meter a Phase. One run of the Agent with one prompt left it to the prompt how much work went to sub-agents, and a real Exhaustive Scan ended as one agent. The Runner app now runs `recon`, `hunt`, `check` and `report` as four runs of `claude`, in order, each on the Phase prompt the engine put in the job (`phasePrompts`, #157, built for the Runner's place). The app keeps its permission model: its deny rules, its tool allowlist and accept-edits mode, and never the flag that switches permissions off, because a person's computer has no container and the repository is untrusted. The engine stores every Runner Scan as `full`; there is no flag any more.
- **One pool, held by the app.** The cap is the lower of the job's `profileCapUsd` and the Runner's ceiling (`budget_for`). The app starts each Phase with `--max-budget-usd` set to the money still left: the cap less the `total_cost_usd` that the earlier Phases reported in their own final JSON results. Nothing moves between Phases, and there is no Report reserve. The agent is told nothing about money, so the Skill cannot mark units `money_exhausted`. The app never writes a `deferred` unit into the agent's ledger; its `PARTIAL:` line is the one in the Partial Scan bullet below.
- **What ends a Runner Scan whose pool is spent.** A Phase that finds nothing left does not start, and a Phase that the limit stops (its result says `error_max_budget_usd`) ends the Scan. A Phase that outruns its slot ends it too. What happens then is the next bullet. A Phase that fails any other way (a crash, a refusal, a result with no cost) ends the Scan as failed with an error that names the Phase and what the agent said.
- **A Runner Scan a limit stops is a Partial Scan (#419).** This reverses the earlier rule here, that a capped Runner Scan ends as failed and sends nothing: a Focused Scan lost its recon and hunt work to the cap in the `check` Phase and the money bought no Report. When a Phase ends on the Budget cap, on a pool with nothing left, or on its time slot, the app sends the files of the Phases so far as a succeeded Scan, if a `coverage-ledger.json` with at least one unit is among them. Without one (a recon that never finished its ledger) the Scan ends as failed, with the cap or time error as before. The app does not edit the agent's files, but before it sends them it does what `check_after` would have done for the stopped Phase: it builds or merges that Phase's `recon-list.json` or `ledger-updates.json` (best effort), then runs the validators on `findings.json` and `coverage-ledger.json`. A `findings.json` that fails is left out and the Report says so, so an unvalidated `findings.json` is never sent; a ledger that fails ends the Scan as failed, because the units it names are the Partial Scan's whole account. It also leaves out a `.json` file the stop cut in half, since the engine refuses a malformed `findings.json`, and it writes `REPORT.md` itself: a first line, then the Phase, the limit, and the `coverage_id` of every unit still `planned` or `in_progress` (the deferred units, 40 listed and the rest counted), then a warning that the Scan does not show the code is free of findings, then a Report the Report Phase had begun. When no unit is missing (the Phase timed out after the ledger was all covered) the first line, after the same prefix, says every unit was covered and names the Phase that did not finish and the ones that did not run, in place of a zero count; the Scan is still stored as partial. The first line is `PARTIAL: money ran out; N units were not covered.` for the cap, the line the engine already reads, so an engine from before this change takes a capped Scan as partial too, and `PARTIAL: a phase ran out of time; N units were not covered.` for the slot, which the engine now also reads (`isPartialScan`). Only a limit does this; the repair run's limit and every other failure still end the Scan as failed.
- **A Runner's Partial Scan weighs 0 in the Verdict, as every Partial Scan does (#419).** There is no new rule: the engine's `scans.partial` column and `scanWeight` already give a Partial Scan weight 0, list it in `scans.ids` and keep its findings open (ADR 0003), and a Partial Scan is not checked against its profile (`profileMet` null). So an unfinished Runner audit can never count toward the Verdict's scan minimum, and a commit whose only Scan is partial reads as not enough scans, never as a pass. Why not a part weight: the unreached units are exactly the ones nobody looked at, and a half weight would let two capped Scans add up to a pass. Findings from the earlier Phases are kept and still block. The cost is the cost the Phases reported, as for any Scan. The page shows the Phase, the limit and the unreached units because they are the Report's own text; a Partial badge in the dashboard stays #67. A failed Scan keeps its work folder on the person's computer, with the files of the finished Phases in it; a Scan that succeeds removes it.
- **The cost the engine sees.** The result carries one `agentLog`, and the engine reads the last JSON object in it. After the last Phase the app adds one line: the last Phase's own result with `total_cost_usd`, `num_turns`, `duration_ms` and the four `usage` token counts summed over every Phase that ran (a failed Scan sends it too). The engine does not change.
- **Time.** The Phases have 90 minutes in all, each with the cloud's slot (10, 45, 25 and 10 minutes). The clone, the validators and the repair run below are outside them. The validators run in the app after the `check` Phase, on `findings.json` and `coverage-ledger.json`; a Phase prompt for a Runner says only that the app runs them, never the single prompt's "do not run any node command", which would keep the agent from starting sub-agents. The `check` prompt says it, and so do `recon` and `hunt` (#410): the skill tells the agent to run its ledger validator after every ledger update, a Runner refuses that, and a `recon` run that was not told spent its 10 minutes on refused commands and a generator script it could not run, then failed. Those two prompts say not to run the validators, `node` or any script, to write the ledger by hand, and that a refused command is expected. When one fails, the app starts one repair run of the agent (a slip such as a slash at the end of a directory path in `starting_paths` should not cost the whole Scan, #156): a short prompt the app builds from the validator's error text, asking it to fix only the errors named, in the files named, in `./out`, and stop. It runs like a Phase: the same permissions and flags, `--max-budget-usd` set to what is left of the Pool, its own log (`agent-repair.log`), its usage summed into the last log line, and a time slot of 5 minutes on top of the 90. Then the validators run again. If they fail again, if the repair run fails or is stopped by the limit, the Scan ends as before: the error names the `check` Phase and the validator's words. If the Pool has nothing left the repair run does not start, and the Scan ends with the cap-reached error. One repair at most for a Scan, and the validators do not run after the `report` Phase. The validators themselves are not loosened, and the agent still may not run `node` on a Runner. The 40-minute limit stays only for a job that carries no Phase prompts. A failure of the environment (Node.js not installed, no `findings.json` or `coverage-ledger.json` written, the skill's check script missing, a check that could not run or did not finish) ends the Scan at once with its own error and starts no repair run: only a validator that ran and reported errors about the agent's files starts one. The engine's wait is longer for a Runner Scan than the cloud's (#157): the 90 minutes, the 10-minute queue timeout and 20 minutes for the clone, the validators and the repair run, 120 minutes (720 polls), inside the 130-minute window.
- **An older pairing of app and engine still works.** A job always carries the single prompt as well. An app that does not know Phases ignores `phasePrompts`, and a new app that gets none runs once on the single prompt, as before, under the 40-minute limit and the old rule (a run the limit stopped after it wrote its report is a Partial Scan).
- **The deploy order is a rule for the profile fields.** The engine's job fields (#144), then the Runner app release that runs the full Skill, then the engine that removes the lite path (#141). An old app that claims a full job runs it under the Runner's own ceiling, not the profile cap. The app release must be cut from a commit before #141 removed the lite Skill folder from its bundle, or be built knowing that a lite job can no longer run. For Phases the order does not matter: the job is additive.
- **The Runner app builds the coverage ledger from the agent's short list (#418).** The skill tells the agent to build the ledger with a script, and a Runner refuses code from the agent, so the agent typed it, one unit for each combination of entry point, boundary, subsystem and attack class. That grows with the repo, and a Balanced Scan on a large repo was still typing it when the recon slot ended. Now the `recon` run writes `recon-list.json` (entries with a surface, a boundary, a subsystem, paths and optionally attack classes, companion blocks and lifecycles) and the app runs `build-coverage-ledger.cjs`, a script of ours next to the skill's validator, on it. The Profile sets the granularity as the skill says: quick uses the one all-in-scope subsystem, deep adds the lifecycles. The agent still runs no `node`; the app runs trusted code it ships, as it does for the validators. The `hunt` and `check` runs return only the units they changed in `ledger-updates.json` (the `coverage_id` and the fields that change), and the app merges them into the ledger; an update may not change a unit's identity, and an ID the ledger does not hold becomes a new unit only when its `canonical_refs` describe one. The app validates the ledger after each build or merge and sets the file aside as `*.applied.json`, so an update is applied once. A problem in the list or the updates is reported in the builder's words and goes to the repair run, which is one for the whole Scan, not one for each Phase. An agent that writes `coverage-ledger.json` itself (an older engine's prompts) still works: with no list or updates the app leaves the ledger alone. The change needs a Runner app release, which carries the script; the engine's prompts for a Runner follow it, and cloud prompts do not change. After a repair run the app checks the ledger again whenever one is there, whether or not a list or updates file is left to apply: a build or merge sets its file aside once it works, so a ledger the validator then rejected would otherwise pass the repair unchecked. A merge reports a malformed new unit (a `lifecycle` that is not a reference, a field in `canonical_refs` that is not one of the five) in its own words, and compares `canonical_refs` field by field, so the order of its keys never reads as a change. **Deploy order: the Runner release first, then the engine.** The engine's prompts for a Runner (`recon-list.json`, `ledger-updates.json`) work only with an app that ships the builder, and nothing can gate this: a job claim carries only the job `sources` the Runner reads, never its version or a capability, so the engine cannot tell an older app and give it the old prompts. An older app that gets the new prompts writes no ledger and its Scan fails at the check ("without writing coverage-ledger.json") after the money is spent. A gate would need a new claim field (a contract change), which this change does not make.
- **The merge computes a unit's `reviewed_paths`, and a Partial Scan keeps the units that validate (#425).** A real Balanced Scan lost its Partial Scan because hunters typed each unit's aggregate `reviewed_paths` by hand and 42 of them disagreed with the unit's checks (the validator wants the aggregate to be exactly the union of the checks' paths). So `merge` in `build-coverage-ledger.cjs` sets `reviewed_paths` of every unit an update touched to the union of its `local_checks[].reviewed_paths`, in order and without repeats, whatever the agent wrote; a unit whose checks are not in that shape is left for the validator. The Partial Scan rule that a ledger which fails validation ends the Scan as failed is narrowed: the app keeps the ledger as it stood before it merged the stopped Phase's updates, and when the merged one fails it puts back the last valid version of each unit the validator names (a new unit that fails is dropped), validates again, and repeats until it passes. The units that validate keep the agent's updates. `REPORT.md` says how many units' updates were not kept: each was kept as it was before the update, or left out if it was new. The validator's output is read, line by line and trimmed, for the `$[index]` of each failing unit. A collision error (a coverage ID used twice, a canonical identity collision, units out of order) can name the later unit, which may be an old one, so it also names the `at $[k]` unit and the app reverts or drops whichever of the two differs from the ledger before the merge. A ledger error that names no unit, a failing unit that no update touched, a pass that changes nothing, or no valid ledger before the merge still ends the Scan as failed with the cap or time error. When the merge itself is refused (an update changes a unit's identity, or a new unit is malformed), none of that Phase's updates are applied, and `REPORT.md` says that Phase's unit updates were not kept. Why: the money and the 13 hunters' valid work should not be lost to a few units the app can put back. The validator is unchanged.
- **A Runner Scan takes no credit** and counts only against `MAX_ACTIVE_SCANS` for its Account, never against the full cap.
- **A Runner runs up to 6 hunters or verifiers at once (#426).** The skill limits agents at once to the number the run prompt gives, or 3 when it gives none; the cloud prompts give none, so the cloud keeps 3, and the Runner's hunt and check prompts give `RUNNER_AGENTS_AT_ONCE` (6). Why: a Balanced Scan of this repository had 13 hunters and ran 5 waves of 3, about 35 minutes plus a critic pass of about 7, most of the 45-minute hunt slot. The risk: 6 agents at once send more requests per minute on the person's own Claude login, which may meet its rate limits sooner. The total work and cost are about the same. The wait-in-foreground rule, the effort, the model and the time slots do not change.
- **The Runner app groups the planned units into even hunter assignments (#427).** The agent grouped a 250-unit ledger itself: it spent about 2 minutes reading it, and with 3 hunters at once it made 13 hunters in 5 waves, the last a single hunter for 6 minutes; with 6 at once it made 6 groups of about 40 units and none had returned after 16 minutes. So after the app builds the ledger from `recon-list.json` (and again after a repair run rebuilds it) it runs `build-coverage-ledger.cjs assign`, which writes `out/hunter-assignments.json`: `{"groups":[{"group","subsystems","coverage_ids"}]}`. The app owns the file; the agent only reads it, and the Runner's hunt prompt tells it to give each group to one hunter and not regroup. The cloud prompts are unchanged and a prompt test pins both. The units are the `planned` ones, sorted by subsystem and then `coverage_id`, and cut into contiguous groups whose sizes differ by at most 1: a subsystem stays together, and a big one is split across neighbouring groups. The number of groups is the units divided by `HUNTER_GROUP_UNITS` (15, in `scan.rs`), rounded up to a multiple of `AGENTS_AT_ONCE` (6, the app's copy of the engine's `RUNNER_AGENTS_AT_ONCE`) unless that would leave groups under half the target size, or more groups than units. A 250-unit ledger gives 18 groups of 13 or 14: 3 even waves. **15 is a first guess to be measured, with the 6: a real Balanced Scan decides whether groups of 10 to 20 return in time.** It was a subcommand of the existing script, not Rust in `scan.rs`, because the script already reads the ledger and its tests run the real `node` with the real validator; the grouping is a pure function of the ledger, so the same ledger always gives the same file. Only the first wave is assigned; units a critic pass adds later are grouped by the agent the same way. The file lies outside the ledger, so `validate-coverage-ledger.cjs` never reads it, and a Partial Scan sends it along as one more file. **Deploy order: the Runner release first, then the engine.** An older app writes no file, so the new hunt prompt would name a file that is not there; a newer app under older prompts writes a file nobody reads.

## Balanced and Exhaustive ask for a separate critic and separate verifiers (#156)

The `standard` and `deep` Phase prompts now ask for sub-agents. The hunt prompt asks for the critic pass to run in a separate sub-agent and be recorded in `critic_passes`; the check prompt asks for each candidate to be verified by a separate sub-agent, whose ID goes in the ledger as the `agent_id` of the check, and says to record `incomplete` with `verification_unavailable` when none can start. The recon and report prompts are unchanged, and `quick` is asked for verifiers only (see below). Why: the prompts only stopped forbidding sub-agents, so a Runner agent could do everything itself, and a real Balanced Scan was stored as unmet (`profile_met: none`) with no critic and no fresh verifier. The sentences are the same in the cloud's place and the Runner's (#157), so this also changes cloud Balanced and Exhaustive Scans: each may start more sub-agents and spend more of its cap, and the cap is not raised (#142 measures it). A prompt cannot force a sub-agent to start: an agent that starts none is still stored as unmet, and the profile check stays the only proof.

`deep` asks for more than `standard`, because it is read against the Exhaustive rows of the profile check (`critic-waves-to-clean`, `final-records-separate-verifier`). The first real Balanced run (a critic after the last wave only, 6 hunters, 3 waves) came out met, but that wording is thinner than the Exhaustive rule. So for `deep` only: the hunt prompt asks for a critic pass with a separate sub-agent after each hunter wave, recorded in `critic_passes`, another wave when a pass accepts something, and a stop when one accepts nothing; the check prompt keeps the per-candidate sentence and the `verification_unavailable` fallback, and adds a final verifier for each record, recorded in `final_verifiers`, that is neither the unit's owner nor a verifier that checked the unit. `standard` and `quick` keep their words. The prompts leave out what the Skill already says: the shape of `critic_passes` and `final_verifiers` entries (`SKILL.md`) and the split of units per lifecycle mode (`RECONNAISSANCE.md`, `SKILL.md`), which is the third Exhaustive row (`units-per-lifecycle-mode`). Each added line costs tokens on every Exhaustive Scan, and more waves and verifiers cost more money. No real Exhaustive Runner run has been made yet (#163).

`quick` (Focused) now also asks for verifiers, in the `check` prompt only: the same three sentences as `standard`, from one constant, with no critic and no final verifiers. Why: the Focused rows of the profile check (`fresh-verifier-per-candidate`, `not-declared-incomplete`) need a check by an agent that is not the unit's owner for every candidate unit, and a real Focused Scan on a big repo had candidates with no fresh verifier, so it read unmet (weight 0). A Focused Scan that finds no candidate has nothing to verify, so the sentence costs it nothing. Same words in the cloud's place and the Runner's. The Focused hunt, recon and report prompts do not change.

## Stopping a Scan (#248)

A person can end a `queued` or `running` Scan on purpose: `POST /scans/:scanId/stop`, behind the same access as starting one. In one transaction the Scan becomes `stopped` (a Scan state of its own, never `failed`) and its Runner job, if it has one, becomes `cancelled`. Why `cancelled`: no Runner claims a cancelled job, and a late report for it is refused, as for a Scan that timed out. A Scan that already ended answers a conflict and changes nothing; a Scan of another Account is not found.

A Stopped Scan keeps no Report and no findings. It frees the Account's active-Scan slot (only `queued` and `running` count), weighs 0 in the Verdict and is never a Branch's latest Scan, because every one of those reads takes `succeeded` Scans only. The Workflow that is still polling can no longer overwrite it: `ledger.mark` never writes over `stopped`, the record step skips it, and a Runner sandbox queues no job for a Scan stopped before the Workflow got to it. The Workflow ends by itself on its next poll, when it finds the job cancelled.

A cloud Scan's Stop (#251) also tears down its Sandbox, which destroys the container, and records the cost the meter had reached. The Scan is charged that cost, as a failed Scan is; a Runner Scan is charged nothing. If the teardown fails the Scan is still stopped, and nothing is charged.

A headless Runner learns of the stop from the job-state route (#250). A Runner built before that keeps running until it finishes, and its result is refused.

Removing a Runner (#252) stops the Scan it is running in the same transaction as the revoke: the claimed job becomes `cancelled` and its Scan `stopped`. The Runner's next job-state check gets a 401, so it kills the agent, sends nothing, keeps its work folder and forgets its token.

Node on Windows has no `O_NOFOLLOW` and no `O_NONBLOCK`, and the upstream checks (`validate-findings.cjs`, `validate-coverage-ledger.cjs`) reject every input when either is missing, so on Windows every check failed with "could not be read as a regular file" (the builder reads through the same function). The Runner app therefore starts each check on Windows with `node --require windows-input-shim.cjs`, our own file that defines both constants and makes the open fail on a symlink or junction. The upstream files stay untouched.
